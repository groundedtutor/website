# Sensitive Access & Password Policy

**Last updated:** August 6th, 2026

This policy explains how {import.meta.env.PUBLIC_SITE_NAME} handles sensitive access during technology support sessions. It is written in plain English so customers, family members, and caregivers understand what we can help with, what we will not do, and how passwords, accounts, private files, and financial information are protected.

This policy applies to in-home visits, phone support, remote support, ongoing support plans, device setup, account help, password manager setup, online safety reviews, backups, file organization, and scam-related support.

# 1. The basic rule

We only access what is needed to complete the service requested. We will explain what we are doing before making important changes, and customers may ask us to pause or stop at any time.

Customers remain in control of their devices, accounts, files, subscriptions, and security settings. Our role is to guide, explain, configure, troubleshoot, and help reduce risk—not to take ownership of customer accounts or make personal decisions for customers.

# 2. Passwords

Whenever possible, customers should type their own passwords. We may guide customers through the process, but we should not need to know or keep customer passwords.

- We do not store customer passwords in notes, text messages, emails, spreadsheets, invoices, booking forms, or customer records.
- We do not keep a copy of a customer’s password manager master password.
- We do not ask customers to send passwords by email, text message, or voicemail.
- If a password must be temporarily visible during a session, the customer should change it afterwards if they are uncomfortable.
- If we create a new password together with a customer, the customer is responsible for saving it somewhere safe.

If a customer cannot remember a password, we can help them use official account recovery options where available. We cannot guarantee that an account can be recovered.

# 3. Password managers

We can help customers choose, set up, and learn how to use a password manager. A password manager can make account security easier, but it must be handled carefully.

- Customers own and control their password manager accounts.
- Customers must choose and remember the master password or store it safely.
- We do not keep a copy of the master password, emergency access codes, or recovery keys.
- Losing access to a password manager may make it difficult or impossible to access saved accounts.
- If emergency access or family sharing is set up, the customer decides who has access and what they can access.

# 4. Two-factor authentication and recovery codes

Two-factor authentication, sometimes called 2FA or extra sign-in protection, helps protect accounts by requiring an extra code or approval when signing in.

- Customers should enter one-time codes themselves whenever possible.
- We do not store one-time codes, backup codes, recovery keys, or authentication app secrets.
- If backup codes are generated, customers are responsible for saving them somewhere safe.
- If a customer loses access to their phone, email, or authentication app, account recovery may be difficult or impossible.
- We can help set up 2FA, but we cannot guarantee that an account can be recovered if recovery information is lost.

# 5. Banking, payments, and financial accounts

We can help with general online safety, scam awareness, device security, and basic navigation of websites or apps. We do not provide financial advice, and we do not make financial decisions or transactions for customers.

- We do not ask for or store banking PINs, card numbers, full banking passwords, or security answers.
- We do not transfer money, pay bills, buy investments, send e-transfers, purchase cryptocurrency, or approve transactions on a customer’s behalf.
- If customers need to sign in to online banking, they should enter their own credentials.
- If customers believe they are the victim of fraud or a scam, they should contact their bank, local police, and the appropriate reporting authorities.
- We may help customers recognize warning signs and improve account security settings, but we cannot guarantee scam prevention or fraud recovery.

# 6. Private files, photos, documents, and email

Some services may involve personal files, photos, documents, email, contacts, or messages. We will only open or review what is needed for the service.

- Customers should tell us if there are folders, files, photos, emails, or apps they do not want opened.
- We will not copy, save, photograph, or keep customer personal files unless it is necessary for the service and the customer agrees.
- Temporary screenshots, exports, logs, or files should be deleted when they are no longer needed.
- We do not search through personal files, email, messages, or photos out of curiosity.
- If we accidentally see private information, we will treat it as confidential.

# 7. Remote support

Remote support may allow us to see a customer’s screen or control their device with permission. The customer remains in control of the session.

- Customers should close private files, photos, messages, and browser tabs before the session starts.
- Customers may pause or end the remote session at any time.
- We will not record a remote session unless we explain why and the customer separately agrees.
- We will not keep remote access open after the session unless the customer clearly agrees to an ongoing support arrangement.
- Temporary remote access codes or permissions should expire or be removed after the session where the tool allows.

# 8. Family members, caregivers, and representatives

We understand that family members and caregivers may help arrange support. However, an adult customer’s privacy and control over their own accounts must be respected.

- We will not help someone access another adult’s device, email, messages, photos, cloud account, banking account, password manager, or private files without that adult’s clear permission or valid legal authority.
- If a family member books a session for someone else, the customer should normally be present and aware of what is being done.
- If there is disagreement between family members, we may pause or refuse the service until authority is clear.
- If legal authority is claimed, we may ask for appropriate documentation before proceeding.
- We will not help bypass passwords, locks, security settings, or account protections unless proper authorization is clear.

# 9. Backups, transfers, and data recovery

We can help set up backups, transfer files, organize photos, and review backup settings. Backups and transfers reduce risk, but they cannot guarantee that every file will be preserved, restored, or transferred successfully.

- Some files may already be missing, damaged, duplicated, corrupted, encrypted, or stored in third-party accounts.
- Cloud storage, external drives, and backup software may involve third-party terms, costs, and limits.
- Customers are responsible for approving what is backed up, moved, deleted, or reorganized.
- We will not intentionally delete customer data without confirmation.
- For serious hardware failure or complex data recovery, a specialist repair or data recovery provider may be required.

# 10. Scam, fraud, and security support

We can help customers recognize common warning signs of scams, improve device security, review suspicious messages, and understand safer online habits. This support helps reduce risk but cannot remove all risk.

- We cannot guarantee that scams, fraud, malware, or account compromise will be prevented.
- We cannot guarantee recovery of money, files, accounts, or access after a scam or security incident.
- We do not negotiate with scammers or contact suspicious parties on a customer’s behalf.
- We may recommend contacting a bank, credit card provider, device manufacturer, internet provider, police, or fraud reporting service.
- Security advice may depend on the device, account, provider, and information available at the time.

# 11. What customers should do before a session

To protect your privacy and make the session smoother, please prepare by doing the following where possible:

- Have the device, charger, and any accessories available.
- Have account recovery information available, such as a phone, email, or trusted device.
- Close private documents, photos, messages, or browser tabs that are not related to the service.
- Do not send passwords by email, text, or voicemail.
- Tell us if there are private files, folders, apps, or accounts that should not be opened.
- Tell us if another person owns the device or account.
- Ask questions if you are unsure why access is needed.

# 12. When we may pause or refuse service

We may pause, limit, or refuse a service if we believe it would be unsafe, improper, unclear, or outside the scope of our work.

- Someone asks us to access another person’s private account without clear permission.
- Someone asks us to bypass passwords, locks, account recovery, or security protections without proper authorization.
- The request appears connected to fraud, coercion, harassment, surveillance, or misuse of another person’s information.
- The customer seems confused about what is being changed and no appropriate representative is available.
- The work requires legal, financial, medical, cybersecurity, or repair expertise beyond the services offered.

# 13. Customer acknowledgement

By booking or receiving services, you acknowledge that:

- You remain responsible for your own devices, accounts, passwords, files, subscriptions, financial decisions, and security choices.
- You should not share passwords, banking PINs, card numbers, recovery codes, or one-time codes unless absolutely necessary, and you should enter them yourself whenever possible.
- You have authority to request help with the device, account, or information involved.
- You understand that technology support can reduce risk but cannot guarantee complete security, scam prevention, data recovery, or account recovery.
- You may ask questions, limit access, pause the session, or stop the session at any time.
